"Medical esthetics" gets used loosely, but it means something specific: skincare treatments delivered, with clinical-grade equipment and protocols tailored to your skin rather than a one-size-fits-all spa menu. That distinction matters more than most people realize when they're comparing options in Alpharetta.

Here's a breakdown of what medical esthetics actually involves, how it differs from a traditional spa facial, and how to choose the right treatment for your skin at THRIVE.

What Makes a Treatment "Medical Esthetics"?

The defining feature of medical esthetics is clinical oversight. At THRIVE, every esthetic protocol — from HydraFacial to microneedling — is designed and supervised by our Medical Director, Dr. Lori Martin, M.D. That means treatment plans are built around your actual skin history, medications, and goals, not a generic package.

It also means access to equipment and formulations that go beyond what a typical day spa can offer, along with the clinical judgment to know when a treatment isn't the right fit — and what to recommend instead.

Why Matters

Safer Combination Treatments

Layering treatments like microneedling and HydraFacial requires understanding how each affects the skin barrier. reduces the risk of over-treating or triggering irritation.

Personalized Protocols

Serums, boosters, and treatment depth are selected for your specific skin type and concerns — not applied uniformly to every client.

Better Handling of Complex Skin

Patients with rosacea, active acne, or sensitive skin benefit from a provider who can adjust protocols in real time rather than following a fixed script.

Whole-Body Context

Because THRIVE is a wellness clinic first, esthetics treatments can be considered alongside nutrient status, hydration, and recovery — factors that show up in skin health over time.

How to Choose the Right Treatment

If you want visible, same-day results with zero downtime, HydraFacial is usually the better starting point. If your primary concern is textural — acne scarring, enlarged pores, or fine lines — a series of microneedling sessions will typically deliver more durable change, though it comes with a short recovery window.

The most reliable way to decide is a consultation, where your provider can assess your skin directly rather than guessing from a list of symptoms.

Supporting Skin Health From the Inside Out

Topical treatments only address part of the picture. Skin quality is closely tied to hydration and micronutrient status, which is why many THRIVE patients pair their esthetics routine with IV therapy or NAD+ IV therapy for antioxidant support that compounds with their skincare results over time. Chronic dehydration and micronutrient gaps show up on the skin as dullness, slower healing, and more visible fine lines — so addressing them internally tends to make every topical treatment work harder.

How Often Should You Schedule Medical Esthetics?

Frequency depends on which treatment you choose and what you're solving for. HydraFacial is low-commitment enough to fit into a monthly routine, since skin naturally turns over on roughly a 28-day cycle. Microneedling works differently — most patients see the best results from a series of 3 to 6 sessions spaced four to six weeks apart, followed by periodic maintenance every few months.

A common approach at THRIVE is to build a rotating calendar: microneedling a few times a year for deeper renewal, with HydraFacial in between to maintain hydration and glow. Your provider can map out a specific cadence during your consultation based on your skin's response to treatment.

What to Expect at Your First Visit

Your first appointment starts with a skin assessment and a conversation about your goals, medical history, and any medications that could affect treatment selection. From there, Dr. Martin or a member of the clinical team will recommend a starting protocol — often HydraFacial, since it carries no downtime and gives immediate feedback on how your skin responds. Deeper treatments like microneedling are typically introduced once your provider has a clearer picture of your skin's baseline.
